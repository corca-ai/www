Q. Please introduce yourself!

Hello! My name is Bae Hwi-dong, and I am working as a Corca AX team lead. He is also the father of two cute daughters, ages 8 and 4.

The photo you sent is unusual. Can you explain what it is?

I don't do it often now, but until a few years ago, I had three hobbies. It was writing, improvisation, and board games.

The photo was taken during an impromptu acting performance. I have been taking related training consistently for quite some time and have actually performed. These days, I don't participate in practice groups or perform directly, but instead, I often go with my daughter to performances held periodically by the theater company I used to work in.

You talked about writing. Speaking of Hwidong, we can't forget about writing. What made you interested in writing?

In fact, as I spend a lot of time on AI these days, I write a little less than before. Last year, I wrote about 250 blog posts, and this year, I wrote a little over 50 in 5 months. Whether it was last year or this year, most of the topics are AI-related.

I have been interested in writing since I was young. About 20 years ago, I studied writing when I was in the military. I rewrite a piece of writing dozens of times on a notepad, and I even request corrections on my writing teacher’s blog. I studied Korean spelling, deliberately came up with a strange title, and then practiced writing a short article on that topic.

Through this process, my interest in writing grew, and as I won a prize at a school writing contest, I gained confidence that I was good at writing. So from this point on, I started writing consistently.

It's been 9 years since I started writing on my blog in earnest in 2018. In the past, I mainly wrote for myself, but from the beginning of last year, I thought I should create a personal branding. I searched for related books and articles, and from then on, I started writing with a little more awareness that I was not writing just for myself, but that other people were reading it. It was from that time that I changed my writing style to polite. Perhaps by coincidence, from then on, people started reading my writing more, and I was excited to get good feedback, so I started writing more.

In the AI era, writing is also often assisted by AI. What is Hwidong’s secret to writing well? I’m also curious about how you are able to write and share so consistently.

Although I make a living using AI, I have a principle that I do not use AI for writing or presentation materials. I think that the more you get used to having AI write for you, the less opportunities you have to create your own writing style or thoughts. So these days, people with their own language and perspective feel more valuable.

I don't have any special secrets to writing well. I usually take quite a few notes on my phone. I take notes of various things, such as things that came to mind while walking down the street, things that interested me after reading a book, and keywords that came up while talking to AI. Many times, the accumulated thoughts are combined and become material for writing. I sometimes write down ideas and anecdotes that come up during conversations with colleagues or acquaintances and post them on the way home.

In the past, there was pressure to turn a piece of writing into a single result. I found it difficult to write because I thought I had to impress someone or write it with perfection like a series. However, when I started writing by sharing the process rather than the result and revealing my shortcomings, I was able to write much more easily.

One of the honest reasons for writing is ‘I want to get people’s attention and have influence.’ It's the same for me, but there isn't much I can control here. Sometimes, a scribbled article in 30 minutes will receive hundreds of likes, and other times, an article that I've been thinking about for several weeks will receive almost no attention. As I experienced this and continued to write a lot, my impatience about views or reactions almost disappeared. In the end, if you develop the habit of writing good enough articles and posting them consistently, some of them will be well received. So now, rather than being too obsessed with one episode, I place more importance on writing consistently.

Q. Please tell us about what you do at Corca!

The Corca AX team all act as ‘AX Consultants’. The work that Corca AX consultants do can be broadly divided into three dimensions: individual, team, and organizational.

At a personal levelTaking responsibility for creating a product from start to finish using AIDo it. We use AI in product development, and we also create products equipped with AI. We carry out the entire process end-to-end, from planning, implementation, distribution, operation, and promotion. I am currently working on several projects like this at the same time.

At the team levelIncreasing productivity so that Corca’s internal members can focus more on their workDo it. There are times when we provide direct help through pair work, etc., but ultimately, we focus more on helping our members improve their capabilities so that they can solve problems on their own. We save time by supporting appropriate tools and environments for tasks that do not need to be done by humans in the first place.

And at the organizational levelTransferring the products created at the individual level, the experience gained at the team level, and the knowledge gained in the process to external organizations.Do it. We help external organizations become 10 times more productive on their own and provide support by providing coaching or introducing tools as needed.

It is the role of an AX consultant to do this work for individuals, teams, and organizations. I also work as an AX consultant, helping my team as a team lead and recruiting new consultants to create this vision together.

Q. What topic does Corca’s AX team talk about most often these days?

Since we are part of the AX team, we talk about it with our agents the most. We often share useful tips with each other, such as “It worked out well when we tried it this way,” or “This model worked well when we used it this way.” Sometimes, an agent's strange answers or interesting examples become the source of jokes within the team.

Additionally, since all AX team members create their own products, there is a culture of using each other's products and exchanging feedback. I tend to naturally talk about what features I liked and what areas could be improved.

On the other hand, there is also a healthy pressure unique to the AX team. Currently, more than 10 products are being developed within the team, and we worry a lot about which product to focus more on and how to disclose it to the outside world. Although we support each other, we do not avoid whipping and criticizing each other to move in a better direction.
